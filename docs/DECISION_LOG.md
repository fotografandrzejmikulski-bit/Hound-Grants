# HOUND Decision Log

## 2026-09-05 — Submission Hardening

### Decision 1 — English as the canonical proposal language

The grant proposal and public README are maintained in English. Polish source material is treated as research input, not as the submission artifact.

### Decision 2 — No unsupported novelty claim

MAC-Bench materially overlaps with HOUND's original framing and terminology. The project therefore positions novelty around measurable implementation characteristics rather than claiming to invent procedural-compliance benchmarking, CSR, or the Machiavellian Gap.

### Decision 3 — State-first evaluation

Policy-relevant claims should be grounded in explicit environment state, authorization state, approval state, provenance, and immutable event records whenever possible.

### Decision 4 — Deterministic-first grading

Deterministic evaluators take precedence over LLM judges for properties that can be encoded as machine-checkable invariants. Human review is reserved for residual ambiguity and calibration.

### Decision 5 — Honest implementation status

The repository is a grant/research specification package. It does not claim that the full 300-scenario benchmark or executable evaluation harness already exists.

### Decision 6 — Program claims are date-qualified

Publicly advertised Vals Fellowship 2026 information is recorded with date context. The advertised June 30, 2026 deadline has passed as of September 5, 2026; current application availability must not be implied without a new official announcement.

### Decision 7 — Metrics are diagnostics, not intent detectors

MG is an observable outcome/compliance divergence. It is not evidence of consciousness, motive, maliciousness, or strategic intent.

### Decision 8 — Cross-domain claims require cross-domain evidence

The proposal can describe multiple planned domains, but it must not generalize empirical conclusions beyond the domains, scenario families, and controls actually tested.
